# Durable Workflow vs Temporal

**For PHP and Laravel teams choosing where durable execution should live.**

Durable Workflow offers an embedded Laravel engine, a self-hosted Server, and
managed Cloud capacity. Its specific appeal is a Laravel-native starting point
and a Cloud bill based on provisioned capacity rather than workflow actions.
That is a different tradeoff, not a claim to replace every Temporal deployment.

Our promise is **predictable cost, predictable behavior, predictable operations**.
This comparison explains what that means and where another choice can fit better.

*Written by the Durable Workflow maintainers. Sources checked September 21, 2026.
Pricing can change; linked provider pages remain authoritative.*

## Choose by the shape of your team

| Your priority | Consider Durable Workflow when... | Consider Temporal when... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Predictable operations | You want workflows inside an existing Laravel app, using its database, queue workers, container, and deployment. | You want a separate orchestration service, managed by Temporal Cloud or operated by your platform team. |
| Predictable cost | You prefer a chosen Cloud capacity allocation and a monthly cap, accepting finite throughput and storage. | You prefer consumption billing tied to Actions and stored history, particularly when demand is intermittent. |
| Predictable behavior | PHP, Python, or Rust fit your service-mode application, and you can validate its required semantics against our SDK guides. | Your application already uses Temporal contracts, or needs its other SDKs and operational integrations. |

Those deployment choices are described in the
[Durable Workflow introduction](/docs/introduction/),
[Temporal Service architecture](https://docs.temporal.io/temporal-service), and
[Temporal SDK guides](https://docs.temporal.io/develop).
Do not interpret this table as feature parity or a migration guarantee.

## PHP support is not the differentiator

**Temporal supports PHP.** Its [PHP SDK guide](https://docs.temporal.io/develop/php)
includes workflows, activities, workers, testing, and examples. You do not need
to change application language simply to adopt Temporal.

The narrower gap Durable Workflow addresses is **durable execution embedded in
a Laravel application's own execution and storage model**. The
[embedded quickstart](/docs/quickstart/) uses Laravel application classes and
queue workers. There is no separate Durable Workflow Server to operate in that
mode. Your database, queues, backups, and application deployment still need
operation; embedded does not mean infrastructure-free.

When you want an independent runtime, the
[PHP SDK](https://php.durable-workflow.com/) connects to Server or Cloud. Its
Laravel bridge supplies container integration, configuration, and logging.
Embedded code and SDK code have different APIs: moving between them is a
deliberate application change, not a promise that existing histories can be
moved unchanged.

## Predictable cost: capacity or consumption?

[Durable Workflow Cloud pricing](https://cloud.durable-workflow.com/pricing)
charges for provisioned runtime time, with an active-month minimum and a UTC
monthly cap. Starts, activity attempts, timers, signals, and queries do not add
per-action charges. They still consume the plan's resources. Storage is fixed
per plan; needing more means arranging a whole-plan upgrade, not an automatic
storage overage. Customer worker infrastructure is separate.

[Temporal's current pricing](https://temporal.io/pricing) has **no base monthly
fee** on its entry-level pay-as-you-go offer. Its bill combines Actions, active
and retained storage, and support. The
[detailed pricing documentation](https://docs.temporal.io/cloud/pricing)
lists Developer support at 10% of usage. Consumption billing can be attractive
when little work runs; it is not inherently unpredictable if you model it.

### Two calculations, not a throughput comparison

All figures below are USD, before tax. These are pricing arithmetic, not a
benchmark or a claim that the plans provide equivalent capacity or availability.

**Durable Workflow Cloud Dev:** the published rate is $0.075/hour, a $2 minimum
per active UTC month, and a $50 monthly cap. For one unchanged provisioned
runtime in one UTC month:

```text
No provisioned time: $0
10 billable hours: min($50, max($2, 10 × $0.075)) = $2
100 billable hours: min($50, max($2, 100 × $0.075)) = $7.50
720 billable hours: min($50, max($2, 720 × $0.075)) = $50
```

Dev includes 5 GB of storage and has **no uptime SLA**. Provisioned idle time is
still billed. Deprovisioning ends capacity accrual; it does not undo a minimum
already incurred. See the [billing and plan terms](/docs/polyglot/cloud-control-plane/)
for other plans and lifecycle rules.

**Temporal Developer, an illustrative monthly workload:** assume 1 million
billable Actions, 720 GB-hours of active storage and 720 GB-hours of retained
storage. These storage quantities are assumptions, not an estimate derived
from the Action count. At the documented rates:

```text
Actions:          1 × $50                      = $50.00
Active storage:   720 × $0.042                  = $30.24
Retained storage: 720 × $0.00105                =  $0.756
Usage subtotal:                                = $80.996
Developer support: 10% × $80.996               =  $8.0996
Illustrative total, rounded to cents:          = $89.10
```

This excludes credits, tax, optional features, replicated namespaces, and
customer worker costs. It is not a quote. Temporal's entry-level offer includes
an SLA; Cloud Dev does not. At lower usage Temporal can cost less, and a Durable
Workflow Dev runtime is **not** guaranteed to sustain this example's workload.

### A workflow is not one Action

Temporal documents [billable Actions](https://docs.temporal.io/evaluate/cloud/actions)
including workflow starts, activity starts and retries, timers, signals, and
queries. Replaying workflow history is not itself billed as new Actions.
A successfully started workflow with one successfully executed ordinary activity
and no additional billable operations contributes two Actions, not one. A retry
can change that count; stored history is another billing dimension.

Our capacity model removes that billing multiplier, not the underlying work.
If retries or retained history exhaust your allocation, you must address the
workload, retention, or plan size. Neither model means unlimited execution.

## Predictable operations: say which deployment you mean

| Deployment | What you operate |
| --- | --- |
| Durable Workflow embedded Laravel | Laravel app, queue consumers, database, and the associated backups and monitoring. |
| Durable Workflow self-hosted Server | Server, its database/cache and operational processes, plus your SDK clients and workers. |
| Durable Workflow Cloud | Your application clients and workers; we operate the selected managed runtime. |
| Temporal self-hosted | Temporal Service and its persistence/visibility configuration, plus your application workers. |
| Temporal Cloud | Your application workers and clients; Temporal operates its managed service. |

Compare the [Server deployment guide](/docs/polyglot/server/),
[Cloud ownership boundary](/docs/polyglot/cloud-control-plane/), and Temporal's
[self-hosting guide](https://docs.temporal.io/self-hosted-guide) and
[Cloud overview](https://docs.temporal.io/cloud).
We do **not** claim that Durable Workflow has no cluster in every deployment,
that Temporal requires Kubernetes, or that Temporal Cloud makes you operate
Temporal Server. Temporal also provides a single-binary local development server;
local setup is not the same as a production availability design.

## When to choose which

Choose Durable Workflow when the embedded Laravel path fits your existing
application, or when PHP/Python/Rust service-mode workers and a finite managed
capacity budget fit your workload. Measure that workload before selecting a plan.

Choose Temporal when its SDK coverage, integrations, existing team expertise,
or managed consumption model match your needs. A working Temporal deployment
is not a reason to migrate by itself. Evaluate required behavior and operational
support, not a feature-count table.

We have not run a controlled cross-product latency or throughput benchmark for
this page, so there is no speed or cost-per-completed-workflow claim here.
